# Camera Direction: Primary-Source Research

Research question: for a traversal-focused 3D platformer drawing from *Super Mario 3D World* and *Bowser's Fury*, should the default camera be an assisted player-controlled orbit or an authored spline/zone camera?

## Recommendation

Use a hybrid, with **assisted orbit as the default in exploratory/free-roaming spaces** and **guided cameras in authored linear traversal, precision-platforming, and set-piece zones**. Treat a guided zone as a temporary composition assist, not a permanent removal of player agency: retain a camera-reset action, preserve limited look input where the shot permits it, and let players reduce or disable automatic recentering.

This is a design inference from the primary sources, not a claim about Nintendo's internal implementation. Nintendo's developers said *3D World* centered on a parallel camera at a set distance and direction, observed that a non-revolving view reduced the chance of getting lost, and described its optional controllable camera as an advanced-player feature; they also said courses could be cleared without mastering camera angles ([Nintendo, “Iwata Asks: Super Mario 3D World,” p. 3](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wiiu/super-mario-3d-world/0/2/)). By contrast, Nintendo describes *Bowser's Fury* as a “free-roaming adventure,” and its own player tips explicitly tell players to use the Right Control Stick to look around from high points ([official *Bowser's Fury* site](https://supermario3dworld.nintendo.com/bowsers-fury/); [Play Nintendo tips](https://play.nintendo.com/news-tips/tips-tricks/tips-tricks-super-mario-3d-world-bowsers-fury/)). The useful distinction is therefore authored/linear traversal versus exploratory traversal, rather than one camera model for the entire game.

## What the first-party Mario sources establish

- The *3D World* team deliberately ruled out some camera types because multiplayer had to work on one screen, then chose a parallel camera that stayed at a set distance and direction from Mario; Nintendo's interview connects that non-revolving view with a lower chance of getting lost ([Nintendo developer interview](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wiiu/super-mario-3d-world/0/2/)).
- The shipped Wii U manual documents pan, zoom, camera-height adjustment, camera reset, and an optional gyro-driven Free Camera Mode; it also states that Free Camera Mode is unavailable in some areas ([Nintendo Wii U electronic manual, pp. 14–16](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/downloads/games_8/emanuals/wii_u_6/super_mario_3d_world_3/ElectronicManual_WiiU_SuperMario3DWorld_EN.pdf)).
- Nintendo's official *Bowser's Fury* description establishes free-roaming travel among islands, while Nintendo's tips establish player-controlled right-stick look-around ([official game page](https://supermario3dworld.nintendo.com/bowsers-fury/); [official tips](https://play.nintendo.com/news-tips/tips-tricks/tips-tricks-super-mario-3d-world-bowsers-fury/)).

These sources support the high-level split above, but they do **not** document Nintendo's collision probes, occlusion solver, recentering curves, zone boundaries, or Bowser's Fury camera state machine. No implementation-specific claim about those systems should be reverse-engineered from marketing copy, a manual, or gameplay footage.

## Cinemachine 3.1: what each option actually provides

| Concern | Assisted orbit | Guided spline/zone |
|---|---|---|
| Position model | `Orbital Follow` can place the camera on a sphere or a three-ring spline surface around its Tracking Target; an Input Axis Controller lets player input drive the orbit ([Unity 3.1 Orbital Follow](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineOrbitalFollow.html)). | `Spline Dolly` restricts camera position to a predefined spline; its position can be authored directly or advanced automatically at fixed speed, nearest point to the Tracking Target, or by a custom method ([Unity 3.1 Spline Dolly](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineSplineDolly.html)). |
| Assistance | Orbital Follow exposes horizontal, vertical, and radial axes plus per-axis recentering; recentering has a center, post-input wait, and completion time, and the horizontal center can be relative to a parent, Tracking Target, or Look At target ([Unity 3.1 Orbital Follow](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineOrbitalFollow.html)). The Input Axis Controller adds per-axis gain and acceleration/deceleration, and can suppress input during blends ([Unity 3.1 Input Axis Controller](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineInputAxisController.html)). | Spline Dolly exposes position and angular damping, but Unity warns that nearest-point auto-dolly can be unstable on arc-like splines: small target motion can cause large camera motion when several spline points are similarly near ([Unity 3.1 Spline Dolly](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineSplineDolly.html)). |
| Zone switching | Different orbit configurations can be separate Cinemachine Cameras. The Brain chooses the active camera with the highest priority, and gameplay events can change priorities or GameObject activation ([Unity 3.1 camera control and transitions](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/concept-camera-control-transitions.html)). | The same priority/activation mechanism can select an authored zone camera, and the Brain can cut or blend between cameras; a blend interpolates camera position, rotation, and other settings over time ([Unity 3.1 camera control and transitions](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/concept-camera-control-transitions.html)). The trigger-volume/zone policy itself remains game-authored logic rather than a camera-design decision supplied by Spline Dolly. |
| Occlusion/collision | `Decollider` pulls a camera out of intersecting geometry but explicitly does not preserve target line of sight ([Unity 3.1 Decollider](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineDecollider.html)). `Deoccluder` instead post-processes camera position to preserve line of sight, can pull forward or seek an alternate view, and exposes smoothing, minimum occlusion time, and separate damping while entering/leaving occlusion ([Unity 3.1 Deoccluder](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineDeoccluder.html)). `Third Person Follow` is another possible rig and includes its own collision resolution, though input must rotate a tracking target rather than the component directly ([Unity 3.1 Third Person Follow](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineThirdPersonFollow.html)). | Decollider and Deoccluder can also post-process a guided camera's final position; both require collider volumes and use physics queries with a documented performance cost ([Unity 3.1 Decollider](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineDecollider.html); [Unity 3.1 Deoccluder](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineDeoccluder.html)). Because Deoccluder can move the camera away from its ideal shot, every guided zone still needs authored occlusion testing. |

## Comfort and accessibility requirements

- Unity's own accessibility course says that mismatch between character movement and camera movement can cause motion sickness, used a static camera by default to avoid unnecessary movement that could be uncomfortable, made dynamic camera motion optional, and recommends small changes plus testing because fast camera movement can disorient players ([Unity Learn, “Camera system”](https://learn.unity.com/course/practical-game-accessibility/unit/design-and-development/tutorial/camera-system?version=2022.3)).
- The same Unity course requires essential information to remain visible in its static mode and explicitly says design answers are not a substitute for regular testing and feedback from players with disabilities ([Unity Learn, “Camera system”](https://learn.unity.com/course/practical-game-accessibility/unit/design-and-development/tutorial/camera-system?version=2022.3)).
- Microsoft's Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117 recommends adjustable horizontal and vertical sensitivity, the ability to disable automatic camera movement, adjustable FOV, and disabling or reducing camera shake, bob, and motion blur; its auto-centering example exposes enable/disable, delay, and speed independently ([Microsoft XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)).
- Microsoft's input guideline recommends in-game remapping, independent X/Y inversion, per-control sensitivity adjustment, digital alternatives where analog input is required, and avoiding a hard requirement for two analog sticks ([Microsoft XAG 107](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/107)).

For CrazyMarket, that evidence translates into the following acceptance criteria:

1. Provide horizontal/vertical look sensitivity, independent axis inversion, camera reset, and recenter **Off / delay / speed** controls. Offer digital camera-turn/reset alternatives so traversal is not gated on simultaneous two-stick use ([Microsoft XAG 107](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/107); [Microsoft XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)).
2. Provide a reduced-motion path: disable shake and other secondary camera motion, reduce or disable automatic recentering, and avoid fast automatic zone blends; validate the result with motion-sensitive and disabled players ([Microsoft XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117); [Unity accessibility camera tutorial](https://learn.unity.com/course/practical-game-accessibility/unit/design-and-development/tutorial/camera-system?version=2022.3)).
3. In a guided zone, frame every required landing, hazard, objective, and exit without demanding camera input; where that cannot be achieved, remain in assisted orbit instead. This applies Unity's stated static-camera requirement that essential information remain in view ([Unity accessibility camera tutorial](https://learn.unity.com/course/practical-game-accessibility/unit/design-and-development/tutorial/camera-system?version=2022.3)).
4. Tune occlusion as a motion system, not merely a visibility check: use selective collision layers, a small viable camera radius, minimum occlusion time, smoothing, and separate entry/return damping, all of which are exposed by Deoccluder ([Unity 3.1 Deoccluder](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineDeoccluder.html)).
5. Reject or reshape spline layouts where nearest-point selection becomes ambiguous, especially arcs around a play space, because Unity documents the possibility of disproportionately large camera moves from small target moves in that geometry ([Unity 3.1 Spline Dolly](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine%403.1/manual/CinemachineSplineDolly.html)).

## Prototype decision test

Build one representative route in both forms and compare: missed landings caused by framing, manual camera corrections per run, occlusion-induced camera displacement, zone-transition reversals at boundaries, and player-reported disorientation. The primary sources provide components and accessibility guardrails, but no first-party source found provides a universal numerical threshold for a “comfortable” blend, recenter delay, damping value, or orbit speed; those values must come from testing the actual movement speeds, geometry, display setup, and intended audience.
